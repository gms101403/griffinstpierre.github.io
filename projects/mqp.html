Pickleball Biomechanics MQP

Development of a wearable system designed to investigate biomechanical loading and movement during pickleball, combining mechanical design, sensors, motion analysis, and experimental testing.

Overview

This project investigated the biomechanics of pickleball movement and loading through the development of a wearable measurement system. The goal was to capture useful biomechanical information while allowing the athlete to move naturally during sport-specific activity.

Problem & Motivation

Pickleball involves rapid changes in direction, acceleration, deceleration, and lower-limb loading. These movements can create substantial mechanical demands on the body, but capturing sport-specific biomechanics outside of a laboratory environment presents a measurement challenge.

The goal of the project was to develop a wearable measurement approach that could capture meaningful biomechanical information while allowing the athlete to perform realistic pickleball movements.

This required balancing measurement accuracy and repeatability with the practical constraints of a wearable system, including comfort, attachment, mechanical stability, and unrestricted movement.

System Development

A key component of the system was a wearable heel-cuff device designed to measure the angle of the tibia relative to the ankle. The device was designed to remain attached to the athlete during movement while providing a repeatable measurement of lower-limb motion.

Developing the wearable system required consideration of the device's geometry, attachment to the body, sensor positioning, mechanical stability, and the ability to collect measurements without unnecessarily restricting natural movement.

Testing & Analysis

Testing incorporated biomechanical measurement techniques including motion capture, force measurement, and sensor data. MATLAB was used to process and analyze collected data to investigate movement and loading patterns.

Engineering Lessons

The project provided experience translating a biomechanical research question into a physical measurement system. It also reinforced the importance of experimental design, sensor integration, data quality, and validating measurements against known or independent methods.

Future Development

Future work could improve the wearable system through additional validation, improved sensor integration, increased sampling reliability, and expanded testing across different movements and athletes.
