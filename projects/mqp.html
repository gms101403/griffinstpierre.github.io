Pickleball Biomechanics MQP

Development of a wearable system designed to investigate biomechanical loading and movement during pickleball, combining mechanical design, sensors, motion analysis, and experimental testing.

Overview

This project investigated the biomechanics of pickleball movement and loading through the development of a wearable measurement system. The goal was to capture useful biomechanical information while allowing the athlete to move naturally during sport-specific activity.

Problem & Motivation

Pickleball involves repeated acceleration, deceleration, changes of direction, and rotational movements. Understanding the mechanical loading associated with these movements can help provide insight into performance and injury mechanisms.

Existing laboratory measurement systems can provide valuable biomechanical data, but they may limit natural movement. This project therefore explored a wearable approach to collecting biomechanical information during realistic pickleball activity.

System Development

The project involved developing and integrating mechanical and sensing components into a wearable system. Design decisions considered sensor placement, attachment to the body, repeatability, movement, and the practical constraints of collecting data during athletic activity.

Testing & Analysis

Testing incorporated biomechanical measurement techniques including motion capture, force measurement, and sensor data. MATLAB was used to process and analyze collected data to investigate movement and loading patterns.

Engineering Lessons

The project provided experience translating a biomechanical research question into a physical measurement system. It also reinforced the importance of experimental design, sensor integration, data quality, and validating measurements against known or independent methods.

Future Development

Future work could improve the wearable system through additional validation, improved sensor integration, increased sampling reliability, and expanded testing across different movements and athletes.
