Drop-Foot Device

Design and prototyping of a mechanical assistive device intended to support foot clearance during walking for individuals experiencing drop-foot.

Overview

Project Overview

This project focused on the design and development of a mechanical device intended to assist individuals experiencing drop-foot during walking. The project explored how mechanical components could be used to influence foot motion and improve functional movement.

The development process involved translating a biomedical need into a mechanical system, developing the design in CAD, producing physical prototypes through additive manufacturing, and evaluating the resulting mechanism.

The Engineering Challenge

Designing an Assistive Solution for Drop-Foot

Drop-foot can make it difficult to adequately clear the foot during the swing phase of walking. This can interfere with normal gait and increase the difficulty of walking safely and efficiently.

The goal of the project was to explore a mechanical solution that could provide assistance while considering the interaction between the device and the user's natural movement.

Developing an assistive device required consideration of both mechanical function and the way the device would interact with the lower limb. The design therefore needed to balance mechanical motion, physical fit, manufacturability, and the practical requirements of a wearable system.

System Design

Translating the Concept into a Mechanical System

The design process involved developing a mechanical concept, translating the concept into a CAD model, and producing a physical prototype for evaluation.

Design considerations included the geometry of the device, its interaction with the lower limb, mechanical motion, manufacturability, and the practical requirements of an assistive wearable device.

Prototyping & Manufacturing

From CAD to Physical Prototype

The prototype was fabricated using additive manufacturing to evaluate the physical design and identify opportunities for improvement. Producing the design as a physical prototype allowed mechanical behavior, fit, and usability considerations to be evaluated beyond the CAD environment.

Physical Prototype Development

Physical prototyping provided an opportunity to evaluate the design beyond the digital CAD environment. The printed components allowed the mechanical structure, fit, and interfaces to be examined as a physical assembly and informed subsequent evaluation of the device.

Testing & Evaluation

Evaluating Mechanical Performance

The prototype was evaluated to assess its mechanical behavior and ability to provide the intended assistance. Testing helped identify design limitations and areas where the mechanism could be refined.

Prototype Evaluation

Evaluating the physical prototype provided insight into the mechanical behavior of the device and highlighted the importance of assessing an assistive mechanism under physical conditions rather than relying solely on CAD development.

Engineering Lessons

Applying Mechanical Design to Assistive Technology

This project reinforced the importance of designing assistive devices around both mechanical requirements and human movement. It also provided experience translating a biomedical need into a physical mechanical system and iterating on a design through prototyping.

The project demonstrated how CAD development, physical prototyping, and mechanical evaluation can be combined to develop and refine a device intended to interact directly with the human body.

Future Development

Continuing Development

Future development could focus on improving the device's mechanical efficiency, comfort, adjustability, and performance during realistic walking conditions.

Additional development and testing could also be used to evaluate the device under a broader range of movement conditions and further refine the interaction between the mechanical system and the user's lower limb.
